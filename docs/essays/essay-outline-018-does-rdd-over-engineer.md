# Does RDD Over-Engineer? Calibration as the Load-Bearing Question
*2026-05-15*

## Abstract

The user-filed open question — *does RDD over-engineer?* (Reflection 016; domain-model.md:984) — resolves under research into a calibration question across multiple methodology layers, not a structural rejection of any single layer. The literature's proportionality principle (Cockburn 2004; Fowler 1999/2015; Simon 1996; Pirolli & Card 1999) and RDD's own corpus evidence (Cycle 018's specialist-subagent dispatch profile; the methodology's prior partial answers) converge on the same diagnosis: the methodology's structural posture is calibrated for high-criticality novel work the methodology actually does, and the open over-engineering risk is per-cycle and per-artifact-layer calibration. Two existing methodology moves (ADR-091 dual-mode BUILD; ADR-083 cognitive economy for human-facing artifacts) are partial answers in the same calibration pattern. The cycle names the pattern explicitly and identifies the next two open calibration moves (audit-infrastructure round count; agent-facing artifact admissibility) plus two additional candidates (per-cycle early-phase chain weight; RESEARCH patch-switching criterion).

**CONCLUSIONS:**

- **C1.** The over-engineering question resolves into a calibration question across methodology layers — not a structural rejection of any single layer. The methodology's posture is supported by the literature for the work the methodology actually does; the open risk is per-cycle and per-layer calibration, not structural posture.
- **C2.** The methodology has been progressively building the calibration pattern across cycles (ADR-091 dual-mode BUILD at Cycle 018; ADR-083 cognitive economy for human-facing artifacts at Cycle 017). The cycle names the pattern explicitly and identifies the next open calibration moves.
- **C3.** Branch B remedy candidate: trim the audit round-count when convergence signals saturation (3 rounds default; 4–5 only when warranted by signal). Literature supports 3–5 productive rounds for critical content; RDD's R1→R4 pattern sits at the upper edge. Solo context removes the knowledge-transfer mechanism, leaving defect-detection as the sole load-bearing justification.
- **C4.** Branch E remedy candidate: an admissibility criterion for agent-facing artifacts (ADRs, scenarios, domain model, system design) parallel to but distinct from ADR-083's Outcome Test for human-facing artifacts. The literature confirms the corpus is in the over-the-consumption-limit territory for LLM agents; Fowler's spec-vs-context-document distinction provides the taxonomy.
- **C5.** Branch D (explicit RESEARCH patch-switching criterion per Pirolli & Card 1999) and Branch A (early-phase chain calibration, reframed) are both promoted to in-cycle ADR candidates. Branch D was promoted at the F-P3 framing finding. The entry-side operationalization (when does a problem warrant RDD?) remains felt-judgment; this gap is real but separately scoped from the calibration moves named above.
- **C7.** C7 refines C1's calibration framing by naming a specific mechanism: the over-engineering failure mode, where it occurs, traces to **research-surveyed possibilities surviving into ADRs without empirical filter** (PLAY evidence; stakeholder-articulated demand; spike validation). Loop 6's cross-project investigation of three external RDD applications (zani, llm-orc, plexus; ~26 ADRs sampled in depth) shows YAGNI discipline is structural across the corpus; kitchen-sink risk is localized to research-driven cascades **lacking empirical filter**. Empirically-grounded elaboration (PLAY-driven, DISCOVER-driven via stakeholder pull, spike-validated) earns its weight. Single-cycle anchor evidence suggests auto-mode BUILD does not cause kitchen-sink in the observed case; the mechanism appears to operate upstream at DECIDE. The user's "what is GOOD about RDD is its propensity to work with nuance" is structurally preserved — research should surface nuance; the remedy is filtering research-surveyed possibilities through empirical grounding at DECIDE.
