## Interpretation

### What the signals collectively suggest

The DISCOVER phase shows a recognizable pattern: the two Grounding Reframe targets from RESEARCH were surfaced faithfully at the DISCOVER opening (structurally correct), the practitioner answered both (gate functioning), and the agent then encoded the practitioner's answers as settled resolutions rather than as input requiring comparative examination (the canonical DISCOVER failure mode from the dispatch brief).

The pattern differs from the RESEARCH phase's pattern in an important way. In RESEARCH, framing adoption was agent-generated (the "calibration question" frame was produced at Loop 1 synthesis) and the practitioner ratified it. In DISCOVER, the framing adoption is practitioner-generated — the practitioner introduced new language, and the agent absorbed it. This is a structural inversion of the RESEARCH susceptibility, and it is in some respects harder to detect: when the practitioner is the framing source, encoding their language looks like user-voice compliance, not framing adoption. The dispatch brief anticipated this: "research-essay framings inherited into product-facing language without being tested against user voice" is the canonical pattern, but its inverse — practitioner-originated framings encoded without alternatives being examined — is equally a susceptibility signal at the DISCOVER boundary.

**The scope-discipline framing:** The practitioner's DISCOVER-opening language about "scope-discipline at delivery" and "exciting as felt-criterion" is new vocabulary that arrived without the examination process the RESEARCH phase applied to agent-generated claims. The research phase ran loops, ran framing audits, and caught the calibration frame's self-justification lean. The practitioner's scope-discipline framing arrived at a gate response and was encoded in approximately three artifact locations within the same phase. The fixed-cost framing that the Grounding Reframe was specifically designed to test against does not appear in the jobs section or vocabulary table, even though the grounding action had been described as: "Does the cross-project evidence foreclose the fixed-cost framing, or does it remain live?" The practitioner's answer — a third frame — was treated as foreclosing the question rather than as adding a dimension to it.

**The Target 2 resolution:** The "DECIDE primary" answer to Target 2 (PLAY-vs-DECIDE as remedy location) was brief. The artifact's Assumption Inversions table records the llm-orc PLAY evidence as "remaining valuable as W7.2 support without relocating the remedy." This is an accurate description of the RESEARCH phase's analysis — but the Target 2 grounding action was designed precisely because that analysis was agent-generated and the practitioner's view had not been surfaced against the PLAY evidence comparatively. The practitioner's "DECIDE primary" answer received the same encoding treatment as the scope-discipline framing: accepted, recorded as resolved, artifact updated.

**The W7.4 vocabulary entry:** The "Nuance preservation (commitment)" vocabulary entry (line 616) is the most structurally interesting artifact in this phase. The entry correctly records that the susceptibility snapshot flagged the elevation as constraint-satisfaction-dressed-as-warrant-evidence. But by encoding the corrective framing as a Product Vocabulary entry, the phase has elevated what is correctly a constraint (remedies must not compress RESEARCH) to a domain concept the MODEL phase will be asked to name. The R3 audit's finding was that W7.4 is a constraint-satisfaction argument performing a framing function that forecloses the PLAY-as-filter alternative. The vocabulary entry preserves the correction but also makes the constraint a first-class concept that downstream phases will define, which gives it more architectural weight than the correction-via-limitation-acknowledgment intended.

**Is the methodology voice vs. user voice criterion met?** The dispatch brief asked whether research-essay framings propagated into product-facing language without being tested against user voice. On the specific question of methodology vocabulary, the artifact uses terms like "Tier-2 methodology-principle level," "Skill-Structure Layer," "Architectural Isolation," and "susceptibility snapshot" extensively in the value tension entries and vocabulary table. The document's header asserts it is written in user voice. For the artifact's stated primary stakeholder — the Solo Developer-Researcher — these terms are appropriate: this stakeholder uses RDD and understands its vocabulary. But the value tension entries for ADR-019.1 through ADR-019.4 read more like research-to-DECIDE forwarding notes than product-facing language. The calibration-moves entries cite specific paper-author-year combinations, LOC counts from cross-project investigations, and essay-outline conclusion references. A practitioner returning to this artifact after time away would need to decode the methodology vocabulary before they could engage with the tension. This is a moderate signal — not a strong one, because the Solo Developer-Researcher is the explicit primary audience — but it is worth flagging because the artifact's stated user-voice criterion does set an expectation that the tensions read in practitioner language rather than methodology-analyst language.

### Pattern assessment — earned confidence vs. sycophantic reinforcement

The DISCOVER phase did not exhibit sycophantic reinforcement in the classic sense (the agent validating the practitioner's views without examination). The two Grounding Reframe targets were surfaced; the practitioner was asked directed questions; responses were recorded. What the phase exhibits instead is a subtler pattern: the agent absorbed practitioner-originated language with the same uncritical fidelity that the RESEARCH phase audit machinery was designed to prevent for agent-originated language. The research-methods reviewer audits research questions; the argument auditor audits agent-produced claims; the susceptibility snapshot audits phase-boundary transitions. None of these mechanisms audits practitioner-originated framings that arrive at gate responses. The gate is the designated site for such framings — but the gate's AID cycle (Attend-Interpret-Decide) is designed to respond to practitioner engagement with the artifact, not to evaluate the epistemic status of the practitioner's own contributions.

The prior snapshot asked: "Is the Tier-2 endorsement a third application of 'the evidence is thin' reasoning to limit methodology enforcement scope within this cycle?" This snapshot can now assess the discover-gate Tier-2 endorsement in context. The discover gate's endorsement (on cross-project-evidence grounds, asserting practitioner agency as the load-bearing element) is consistent with both (a) a coherent application of C7's criterion (thin evidence supports vocabulary, not enforcement), and (b) a self-limiting pattern in which every consequential scope choice in the cycle reduces methodology overhead at that decision. The cycle has now made this choice at three junctures: the R3 framing finding downgrade of ADR-019.4 (from Skill-Structure to Tier-2), the practitioner's gate response on Target 1 (scope-discipline as the frame, which forecloses structural entry-cost as a separate mechanism), and the discover-gate endorsement of Tier-2 on cross-project-evidence grounds. All three are individually defensible. The aggregate pattern — every scope-limiting choice at every consequential juncture in a cycle investigating whether RDD over-engineers — is the self-justification lean F-P2 named at RESEARCH, now carried through three consequential decisions. The honest framing from the prior snapshot still applies: "coherent does not mean correct."

### Comparison to prior snapshot

The prior (RESEARCH-phase) snapshot identified two principal findings carrying into DISCOVER: (1) the calibration-frame's agent-generation provenance and the fixed-cost alternative never returned to; (2) the W7.4 elevation performing a framing function that forecloses PLAY-as-filter without examination. The DISCOVER phase addressed both by surfacing them — which the prior snapshot recommended — but resolved both by accepting the practitioner's brief gate responses without comparative examination. The trajectory from RESEARCH to DISCOVER is therefore: the finding was surfaced (improvement over RESEARCH, where it was only identified as an open question), the practitioner was given agency to answer (correct), the answer was encoded without alternatives being examined (the pattern the DISCOVER opening was designed to prevent). The cycle has demonstrated its own mechanism partially — gate asks the question; practitioner answers — without the full mechanism — agent tests the answer before encoding it.

## Recommendation

**Grounding Reframe recommended — targeted and MODEL-opening applicable.**

Three findings warrant grounding actions at the MODEL opening. A fourth is feed-forward only.

### Grounding Action 1: Belief-map the scope-discipline framing against the fixed-cost framing before MODEL encodes it as domain vocabulary

**What is uncertain:** The practitioner introduced "scope-discipline at delivery" / "minimum useful scope" / "exciting as felt-criterion" at the DISCOVER opening in response to Target 1 (does the fixed-cost framing remain live?). The agent read this as resolving the calibration-versus-fixed-cost question. But the practitioner's answer named a dimension within the calibration frame (scope-discipline at DECIDE), not a response to the fixed-cost alternative. The fixed-cost alternative — that no per-layer calibration dial corrects a methodology whose startup cost is structurally mismatched to lower-stakes problems — was not addressed by the practitioner's answer. The practitioner's scope-discipline framing is compatible with both the calibration frame (calibrate at DECIDE) and the fixed-cost frame (calibrate at cycle entry). The artifact does not disambiguate these, and the MODEL phase will be asked to name domain concepts including "Minimum useful scope" and "Scope-discipline at DECIDE."

**Grounding action:** At MODEL opening, before domain vocabulary is extracted, surface this question to the practitioner: "The scope-discipline framing you introduced at DISCOVER — 'deliver the simplest useful thing consistent with research; exciting as the felt-criterion for scope addition' — is encoded as ADR-019.4 vocabulary. The fixed-cost alternative the grounding action was designed to test would have led to a minimum-viable-problem-size criterion at *cycle entry*, not a scope-minimality felt-trigger at *DECIDE*. Your scope-discipline framing operates within the calibration frame. Does that framing also foreclose the entry-side question — that some cycles shouldn't use full-RDD at all — or is that separately live? If the entry-side is separately live, it should appear in the MODEL domain vocabulary as a distinct concept (entry-side calibration gap), not as a footnote on ADR-019.4."

This is a diagnostic question, not a request to redo DISCOVER. The MODEL domain vocabulary should reflect whichever answer the practitioner gives — but it should not inherit "Minimum useful scope" as a DECIDE-layer concept without the entry-side question being explicitly addressed, because if entry-side calibration is the practitioner's actual concern, the domain concept belongs at cycle entry, not at DECIDE.

**What is being built on without grounding:** Without this check, MODEL encodes "Minimum useful scope" and "Scope-discipline at DECIDE" as domain concepts, and DECIDE drafts ADR-019.4 as a DECIDE-layer vocabulary amendment. If the practitioner's actual concern is entry-side — when does a problem warrant RDD at all — then ADR-019.4 addresses the wrong layer, and the methodology will have committed to a DECIDE-layer amendment that does not address the practitioner's concern. The downstream phases (MODEL, DECIDE, BUILD) will inherit vocabulary built for scope-discipline-at-DECIDE, and the entry-side calibration gap will remain separately scoped in domain-model.md:926 without being addressed by this cycle's amendments.

### Grounding Action 2: Disambiguate the PLAY-vs-DECIDE resolution before MODEL encodes DECIDE-primacy as domain structure

**What is uncertain:** The practitioner answered Target 2 ("DECIDE primary") briefly. The DISCOVER artifact's Assumption Inversions table records this as resolved. But the grounding action was designed because the W7.4 elevation had performed a framing function foreclosing PLAY-as-filter without the PLAY evidence being evaluated against the DECIDE-remedy claim. The practitioner's "DECIDE primary" answer was given without the PLAY evidence being surfaced as a counterweight. Specifically: llm-orc Cycle 5's PLAY pulled features back — this is W7.2 in the Essay-Outline and is arguably the strongest empirical signal in the cross-project evidence for where filtering actually happens. The DECIDE-primary answer was accepted at face value; the PLAY evidence was not presented alongside the question.

**Grounding action:** At MODEL opening, surface this specifically: "The DECIDE-primary resolution for Target 2 was accepted after a brief response at the DISCOVER opening. The cross-project evidence that most directly shows filtering happening includes llm-orc Cycle 5's PLAY pulling features back — reframing observability scope, reducing what Cycle 6 built. That evidence points to PLAY as where filtering *actually happened* in observed cases. ADR-019.4 targets DECIDE. Before MODEL encodes DECIDE-primacy as domain structure, does the PLAY evidence support DECIDE as the intervention site, or does it support a PLAY-strengthening amendment as the primary remedy with DECIDE vocabulary as secondary? This is not a request to change the answer — it is a request to give the PLAY evidence a fair hearing before the MODEL vocabulary reflects a DECIDE-centered frame."

**What is being built on without grounding:** Without this check, MODEL's domain vocabulary will encode the empirical-grounding criterion as a DECIDE-layer concept, and DECIDE will draft ADR-019.4 without having compared it against a PLAY-strengthening amendment. The vocabulary amendment will commit methodology scope to DECIDE without the strongest empirical evidence (PLAY-as-filter, W7.2) having been weighed against an alternative ADR. This is the same foreclosure the R3 framing audit identified, now at the MODEL-phase crystallization point.

### Grounding Action 3: Scope-check the "Nuance preservation (commitment)" vocabulary entry before MODEL defines it as a domain concept

**What is uncertain:** The "Nuance preservation (commitment)" entry in the Product Vocabulary table encodes the W7.4 elevation as a methodology commitment, correctly noting that the susceptibility snapshot flagged it as constraint-satisfaction-dressed-as-warrant-evidence. This is a corrective move within the artifact. But the vocabulary entry will become input to MODEL's domain-vocabulary extraction. When MODEL extracts domain concepts, "Nuance preservation" as a named commitment will likely produce a domain model entry — a glossary term with definition, stakeholder, and invariant implications. The R3 audit's finding was that W7.4 is a value alignment marker, not an independent empirical claim. Encoding it as a Product Vocabulary entry elevates it from a gate utterance to a domain concept, which gives it more architectural load-bearing weight than the correction-via-acknowledgment intended.

**Grounding action:** At MODEL opening, flag this entry explicitly: "The 'Nuance preservation (commitment)' vocabulary entry traces to the practitioner's gate utterance 'what is GOOD about RDD is its propensity to work with nuance.' The RESEARCH audit correctly flagged this as constraint-satisfaction, not evidence — it establishes what remedies must not do (compress RESEARCH), not why DECIDE is the right remedy location. If MODEL extracts this as a domain concept, the concept should carry the same constraint-only framing: 'nuance preservation is a criterion any remedy must satisfy, not a feature of any specific remedy.' It should not become a justification for the DECIDE-layer remedy in the domain model. The distinction is whether the domain entry reads as a property of RDD (RESEARCH surfaces nuance — this is settled) or as a constraint on remedy-design (remedies must not compress RESEARCH — this is also settled). It should not read as evidence that DECIDE is the right remedy site — that question is what Grounding Action 2 asks."

**What is being built on without grounding:** Without this disambiguation, MODEL may encode nuance preservation in a way that produces a domain invariant or constraint that effectively forecloses PLAY-as-filter by treating RESEARCH-nuance-surfacing as the primary value and DECIDE-level-filtering as the canonical remedy. The architecture of the domain vocabulary would then reinforce the DECIDE-primary resolution from Grounding Action 2, compounding both issues at the MODEL phase.

## Feed-Forward Observations for MODEL and Downstream Phases

**Feed-forward 1 (practitioner-originated framing audit gap):** The methodology's audit machinery audits agent-produced claims (citation audit, argument audit, framing audit) and phase-boundary transitions (susceptibility snapshot). It does not have a mechanism for auditing practitioner-originated framings that arrive at gate responses. The DISCOVER phase demonstrates that this is a real gap: the practitioner's scope-discipline language arrived without examination and was encoded in approximately three artifact locations within the same phase. For MODEL, DECIDE, and BUILD, practitioners will continue to introduce framings at gate responses. A lightweight mechanism — a directed belief-mapping question at the artifact-encoding moment, not a full audit — may address this class of framing adoption without adding substantial overhead.

**Feed-forward 2 (three-application F-P2 pattern):** The cycle's self-justification lean (F-P2, named at RESEARCH, carried forward) has now been applied at three consequential scope-limiting junctures: the R3 framing-finding downgrade of ADR-019.4 to Tier-2; the practitioner's gate-response on Target 1 (scope-discipline forecloses entry-side calibration); the discover-gate Tier-2 endorsement. All three are individually defensible against C7's criterion. The aggregate pattern — every scope-limiting choice at every juncture in a cycle about over-engineering — is not provably a sycophantic pattern, but it is the pattern F-P2 predicted. DECIDE should note that all four in-cycle ADR candidates have Tier-2 scope for the two most consequential ones (019.2 Agent Outcome Test and 019.4 empirical-grounding criterion) and Skill-Structure Layer for the two less consequential ones (019.1 audit round-count and 019.3 patch-switching criterion). The Tier-2 assignments are at the two ADRs where enforcement would most directly address the over-engineering critique's mechanisms. Whether this is coherent application of one principle or content-selection producing systematically less methodology-overhead at every consequential scope choice is the question DECIDE should hold explicitly.

**Feed-forward 3 (entry-site question for domain model):** Whether "entry-side calibration gap" belongs in this cycle's domain model as a named concept — or only in domain-model.md:926 as an open question — is the MODEL phase's decision. But the decision should be explicit. If MODEL does not name the entry-side gap, DECIDE will produce four ADRs addressing DECIDE-layer and RESEARCH-layer calibration; the entry-side gap (when does a problem warrant RDD?) remains at felt-judgment. If the distribution-of-cycle-types limitation is load-bearing (as the Essay-Outline §9 asserts), the entry-side concept may be the most consequential thing this cycle could address. The MODEL phase is the right place to assess whether a concept should enter the domain vocabulary — not upstream (where it was correctly identified as separately-scoped) and not downstream (where it will be foreclosed by the four in-cycle ADRs).

**Feed-forward 4 (methodology voice in value tensions):** The Cycle 019 value tension entries (ADR-019.1 through ADR-019.4, the scope-of-claim entries, and the methodology-level observation entries) read as RESEARCH-phase forwarding notes with paper citations and cross-project investigation references. This is not wrong — the Solo Developer-Researcher is the primary audience and understands this vocabulary — but the artifact's stated user-voice criterion sets an expectation. For MODEL's domain-vocabulary extraction, the distinction between "user terms" (the artifact's vocabulary table) and "research findings encoded as value tensions" may matter: domain concepts should trace to user vocabulary, not to research conclusions. The model phase should treat the ADR-019.x value tension entries as research-forwarding material and extract domain concepts from the user-voice language where that language is present — not from the research-forwarding notes themselves.
