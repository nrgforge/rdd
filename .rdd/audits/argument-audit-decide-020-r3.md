### New findings from this round

**P1 — Must Fix**

**1. Location:** ADR-099 §Authority, Option B (the Invariant-3 citation added this round) vs. ADR-100 in full (no mention of the same fact); cross-referenced against `.rdd/gates/020-decide-gate.md` ("Practitioner response to the reframe") and `docs/domain-model.md` Invariants 1 and 3.

**Claim:** ADR-099 now states: "At the DECIDE gate the practitioner added the fact that settles it for this corpus: they have authored none of the 98 ADRs; all were composed by agents in session... The practitioner's contribution to a decision record is the gate ruling and the verbatim statements the record carries as product origin (Invariant 3: generation is pragmatic; the ruling is the epistemic act)." The gate note extends this further, in text that appears nowhere in either ADR: "C is settled on the practitioner's own fact, not on the agent's recommendation... It also reframes the engagement-trajectory observation: the practitioner's contribution to decision records has always been the gate ruling, not authorship, which is what Invariant 3 assigns."

**Evidence gap:** Two gaps compound. First, a cross-ADR handling inconsistency: ADR-100, not ADR-099, is the ADR whose entire subject is whether the practitioner's engagement is sufficient to discharge Invariant 0 (its Context quotes the DISCOVER-gate adjudication-load testimony at length, its §Posture section directly argues this cycle's own engagement pattern, and its Rejected Alternative A is built entirely around "the practitioner stopped using the methodology" as evidence). A fact this directly relevant — the practitioner has never authored any decision-record prose, in 98 instances — appears only in ADR-099's narrower Authority sub-decision and nowhere in ADR-100's engagement argument. Second, and more substantively, citing Invariant 3 answers the wrong question. Invariant 3 classifies a contribution as pragmatic or epistemic; it says nothing about whether a given epistemic-category act was substantively performed. Invariant 1 sets that bar directly: "Every gate must require the user to produce something — an explanation, prediction, reconstruction, or articulation — not merely assess something." A one-character selection among agent-developed, agent-recommended options is structurally closer to the domain model's own named anti-pattern, the Approval Gate ("reviews and accepts... without producing anything... Avoid: this is the pattern being replaced"), than to articulation. The susceptibility snapshot already tested this directly and left it open — finding (i): "Does 'C' meet the earned-confidence test for a non-defaultable choice? No... there is no evidence in the record that the practitioner can name what they would need to believe for A, B, or D to be right" — and finding (iii): the three-gate trajectory (paragraph-length independent reasoning at DISCOVER → "What is 'separation' here?" / "K - makes sense" at MODEL → "C" at DECIDE) is "monotonic decline in engagement depth, not noise." Neither finding is re-engaged by either ADR. The gate note's reframe answers them by re-labeling the category (it is "the epistemic act" per Invariant 3) rather than by addressing whether this particular instance of it was adequately performed — which is the actual question (i) and (iii) raised. The reframe additionally reverses the record's own timeline: "C" was recorded before the authorship fact was elicited (the fact emerged from a belief-mapping test the snapshot specifically requested, aimed at testing whether "C" reflected genuine engagement), so "C is settled on the practitioner's own fact" characterizes a fact that answers why Option B is weaker as if it were also evidence that the practitioner's engagement with A, C, and D was adequate — a claim the fact does not support.

**Recommendation:** State the authorship fact in ADR-100 as well as ADR-099, in the ADR that actually argues the engagement-sufficiency question, and address it against Invariant 1's "must produce something" bar directly rather than only against Invariant 3's pragmatic/epistemic category test. If the corpus's considered position is that a confirmed one-line ruling on agent-developed, agent-recommended options is sufficient production for a "not defaultable" choice, argue that position explicitly (it is a defensible one — Invariant 1 does not specify a minimum length, and a confirmed ruling is not nothing) rather than letting the category label stand in for the argument. Until that argument is made, treat the snapshot's finding (i) as still open, not resolved by the reframe.

## Section 2: Framing Audit

### R2 framing finding — resolution

**Framing P2** (silent-affirmance vs. explicit ruling in the gate record) — **RESOLVED**, per the R2 resolution table above.

### Question 1: What alternative framings did the evidence support?

R1 and R2 already surfaced and adjudicated the major alternative framings for this cycle's ADR structure (one ADR vs. two; driving vs. building posture). This round's fresh material — the DECIDE-gate authorship disclosure — supports one further alternative framing not yet named: **the disclosure could have been read as evidence about the methodology generally, not only about the Authority sub-decision specifically.** Under this framing, "the practitioner has authored none of 98 ADRs" is not a fact that resolves one canonical-source question; it is a data point suggesting the corpus's long-standing theory that prose-authoring forces epistemic engagement (Option B's claimed "strongest merit," but also the older intuition behind requiring the practitioner to write anything at all) may never have applied to this project, at any point, for any ADR — a much larger claim than "Option C beats Option B here." A reader would need to believe that the corpus's engagement mechanism has, in practice, always run through gate rulings rather than authorship, and ask whether other Invariant-3-grounded assumptions elsewhere in the methodology (e.g., that a "required field" or a "rejected-alternatives section" meaningfully forces engagement anywhere, not just in ADRs) rest on the same untested premise. Neither ADR takes up this wider reading; both confine the fact to the Authority sub-decision.

### Question 2: What truths were available but not featured?

The susceptibility snapshot's findings (i) and (iii) — both directly on point, both unresolved by anything in the ADR text — are available in `susceptibility-snapshot-020-decide.md` and are not re-engaged anywhere in either ADR. Finding (i) ("does 'C' meet the earned-confidence test... No") and finding (iii) (the three-gate declining-engagement trajectory, read against the same two boundaries ADR-100 cites as its own validating evidence) are the two most directly relevant pieces of source material to the authorship-disclosure exchange that followed them, and neither is quoted, addressed, or reconciled in ADR-099 or ADR-100. The gate note's reframe answers a different, narrower question (was Option B's specific merit real) and that answer is treated, in the gate note only, as if it closed both open findings. See Section 1 P1 above.

### Question 3: What would change if the dominant framing were inverted?

The dominant framing across both ADRs (and the corpus generally, since Cycle 019) is: *audit-substance preservation protects the practitioner's ability to retrieve full reasoning when they need it, alongside the agent's context needs.* ADR-099's Rejected Alternative E states this directly: "the problem is who has to read them, not that they exist."

Inverting it: *given the practitioner has authored none of 98 ADRs and, on this cycle's own three-gate record, engages with rulings at declining depth, the demonstrated reader of full audit-substance is the agent (in-session and across future audits), not the practitioner — the practitioner's actual point of contact with the corpus is the Surface View and the one-line ruling, not the raw record.* This does not necessarily argue against the architecture (agent-context continuity and future-audit reconstruction are real, independently sufficient justifications — Forward and Lethbridge 2002; Gupta et al. 2026 are cited for exactly this), but it means the practitioner-facing half of the "who has to read them" justification is, on the corpus's own evidence to date, unobserved rather than merely untested. Neither ADR states the narrower, evidenced version of the claim; both retain the broader, unscoped one.

### Framing Issues

**P1 — The DECIDE-gate reframe used a narrow factual correction to close out two distinct, unresolved framing-class findings, without surfacing either back to the practitioner, on the same rule (ADR-100 rule 4) drafted this same phase to prevent exactly that.**
- **Location:** `.rdd/gates/020-decide-gate.md`, "Practitioner response to the reframe" (the "Recorded:" paragraph); cross-referenced against `.rdd/audits/susceptibility-snapshot-020-decide.md` findings (i) and (iii), and ADR-100 rule 4.
- **Claim:** "C is settled on the practitioner's own fact, not on the agent's recommendation... It also reframes the engagement-trajectory observation: the practitioner's contribution to decision records has always been the gate ruling, not authorship, which is what Invariant 3 assigns."
- **Evidence gap:** The practitioner's message answers one narrow question (is Option B's forcing-function merit real) with a fact about ADR authorship. It does not address Options A or D, and says nothing about whether the ruling process itself constitutes adequate engagement with a "not defaultable" choice — the specific question snapshot finding (i) raised and finding (iii) sharpened into a three-gate trend. The "Recorded" paragraph uses the fact to close both threads anyway, and records both conclusions as settled without putting either back to the practitioner as its own item. This is the same shape as the two violations this same gate's own snapshot caught earlier in the same dispatch (the triage table's false claim that A-P2-5's pivot-criterion gloss was surfaced; the false "surfaced" label on F-P1-b) — an agent-authored interpretive leap recorded as settled, exceeding what the practitioner's words support. ADR-100 rule 4, drafted in this same phase, states: "A framing-audit or snapshot finding that names an alternative the artifact did not choose is surfaced with the agent's analysis done and a pivot extracted... It may not be dispositioned 'resolve' without the practitioner seeing it." The engagement-trajectory concern is exactly this kind of finding — it names an alternative reading (the declining depth is a real problem) that the reframe does not choose — and the "Recorded" paragraph functions as a resolve disposition on it, argued and closed by the agent, without the practitioner ever seeing the specific claim being attributed to them. Mitigating factor: unlike A-P2-5 and F-P1-b, this conclusion has not been promoted into the gate note's "Settled premises" list — it sits in interpretive prose only — so there is still a clean opportunity to correct it before ARCHITECT treats it as inherited ground.
- **Recommendation:** Before ARCHITECT proceeds, put the actual claim to the practitioner as its own one-line question, consistent with the mechanism ADR-100 itself specifies: "Does the ruling depth across these three gates reflect that authorship was never your job, so a ruling is enough — or would you want more of your own reasoning in the record from here forward?" This is a one-question check, not a new research loop, and it is the same remedy this audit sequence has recommended for every prior instance of this pattern (A-P1-2, A-P2-5, F-P1-b).

**P2 — What audit-substance is preserved for, going forward, is not stated at the resolution the corpus's own evidence now supports.**
- **Location:** ADR-099 Rejected Alternative E; the general audit-substance-preservation rationale running through ADR-099's binding constraints and Cycle 019's C1 finding.
- **Claim:** "the problem is who has to read them, not that they exist" — stated without qualifying who, in fact, has been reading them.
- **Evidence gap:** See Question 3 above. The corpus's own record (0/98 ADRs authored by the practitioner; declining ruling depth across this cycle's three gates) narrows the answer to "primarily the agent, in-session and at future audits," with the practitioner's demonstrated point of contact being the Surface View and the ruling, not the raw record. Leaving the claim unscoped overstates the practitioner-facing case for the architecture in the same understated way the driving-posture framing already did once this cycle (R1 F-P1-a).
- **Recommendation:** A sentence in ADR-099 naming the narrower, evidenced claim would let a future reader (including a future audit) evaluate the architecture against what it has actually delivered rather than against an implied practitioner-drill-down use case with no observed instance yet. Does not require a decision change.
