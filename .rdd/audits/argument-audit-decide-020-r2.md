**P2 — Should Fix**

**1. Location:** ADR-099 header (`Status:`) and `§Authority`; cross-referenced against `adr-074-adr-supersession-workflow.md` §2 ("ADR mutability rules").
**Claim:** ADR-099's outer status is "Accepted (Conditional Acceptance...)"; nested within its Decision section, `§Authority` carries its own independent status marker — at the moment this ADR was drafted, "Proposed pending the practitioner's ruling," and as of the ruling, "Accepted, option C." Both statuses live inside one document.
**Evidence gap:** ADR-074 defines exactly one Status field per ADR, with the rule "For accepted ADRs: Immutable: Context, Decision... Mutable: Status field." It says nothing about a document carrying two independently-tracked lifecycle states at different points in its own Decision section, and nothing about what happens if a nested sub-decision is still open when the outer document's status already reads "Accepted." In this instance the question resolved cleanly because the practitioner ruled at the same DECIDE gate the document itself was presented at, so by the time any reader (including this audit) sees the file, both statuses are settled together. But the pattern ADR-099 introduces — status granularity below the whole-ADR level — is genuinely new to the corpus and is not licensed or even acknowledged anywhere in ADR-074, the ADR that is supposed to be the authoritative source on what "Accepted" means and what stays mutable after it. A future cycle that leaves a nested sub-decision open *across* gates (rather than resolving it at the same gate it was proposed at, as happened here) would recreate the exact ambiguity this audit flagged on first read of the pre-ruling file: is the whole ADR "Accepted" (making its Decision section, sub-decision included, immutable per ADR-074) or not? ADR-099 relies on ADR-074's authority to reject its own Option A ("re-encoding accepted bodies contradicts ADR-074") without addressing whether its own nested-status construction is itself compliant with the model it invokes.
**Recommendation:** Add a short passage to ADR-074 (via a proper `Updated by` header, since ADR-099 already touches ADR-074 once) naming the convention explicitly: a Decision section may contain a nested sub-decision with its own status, provisional until the same or a later gate resolves it; the outer ADR's status only reaches its full lifecycle meaning once every nested sub-decision is settled. This costs one paragraph and forecloses the ambiguity for the next ADR that nests a sub-decision.

### Question 1: What alternative framings did the evidence support?

**Already covered by R1 and now either adopted (one-ADR framing, in modified two-ADR form) or explicitly re-affirmed (driving posture, re-affirmed rather than reversed).** No new alternative framing at the level R1's three surfaced. One narrower alternative surfaces from this round's own revision: **Authority could have been decided as an ADR-074 amendment directly, rather than as an ADR-099 sub-decision that then produces an ADR-074 header.** Under this framing, the "what does the practitioner edit from now on" question is squarely ADR-074's own subject matter (body-immutability and its unit of application), and resolving it as a standalone update to ADR-074 would have avoided the nested-status question in new finding P2-1 entirely — ADR-074 would simply have gained a new clause, with no intermediate document carrying two statuses. A reader would need to believe that Authority's substantive content (which unit type is canonical) belongs more naturally with the architecture that needs a canonical source (ADR-099) than with the ADR that defines what "canonical" and "immutable" mean generally (ADR-074) — a defensible view, since ADR-099's own C4/W4.4 sourced Authority as one of the six spike constraints, not as an ADR-074 concern per se. One item is available in the source material and still not featured: `020-decide-gate.md` records that **neither pivot-with-a-default was actually answered by the practitioner** — the practitioner's entire response to the gate was "C" (the Authority ruling); both the driving-mode-continuation default and the two-ADR default were accepted by silence, not by an affirmative statement. This is true to the letter of ADR-100 rule 2 (route-level choices are "presented as overridable defaults... the practitioner overrides or does not"), so it isn't a violation. But it is not stated anywhere in ADR-099, ADR-100, or the gate note's own summary sections ("Settled premises," "Commitment gating outputs") that these two commitments rest on silence rather than an explicit ruling — the gate note's "Settled premises" list states "Driving mode continues for ARCHITECT and BUILD of this cycle" with the same declarative confidence as "ADR-099 §Authority: option C," even though the practitioner explicitly answered only the latter.

### Question 3: What would change if the dominant framing were inverted?

The dominant framing across both ADRs is now: *pre-triage is not a black box — it produces a record, and the isolated snapshot is what makes it trustworthy.* Inverting it: *the record and the isolated snapshot are downstream controls; the pivot that decides whether the controls even get exercised on a given item (rule 2's destination/route/engine sort, and the practitioner's silence-as-affirmance on route-level defaults) happens upstream of both, in the same orchestrator context whose output is under audit.*

Under the inverted framing, the "driving mode continues" default is not merely one route-level choice among many — it is the choice that determines how much of everything else in ARCHITECT and BUILD gets sorted into "route" (presented as an overridable default, answerable by silence) versus "destination" (a required ruling). The practitioner ruled on exactly one thing at this gate (Authority, "C") and by silence accepted two structural defaults that both bear on the same question the R1 framing audit's own inversion raised: how much epistemic control does the practitioner retain going forward. That the gate note treats "not overridden" and "ruled C" as equally settled ("Settled premises... Driving mode continues... ADR-099 §Authority: option C") is the place this inversion bites hardest — one is a decision the practitioner made; the other is a decision the practitioner did not make and the process is entitled, by its own new rules, to treat as made anyway.

### Framing Issues

**P2 — The driving-mode-continuation and one-ADR-vs-two pivots were resolved by the practitioner's silence, and the gate record does not distinguish "ruled" from "not overridden" when stating settled premises.**
- **Location:** `.rdd/gates/020-decide-gate.md`, "Belief-mapping question composed for this gate" and "Commitment gating outputs → Settled premises."
- **Claim:** "Settled premises... Driving mode continues for ARCHITECT and BUILD of this cycle" and "Two ADRs, not one or three" are listed alongside "ADR-099 §Authority: option C" as equally settled.
- **Evidence gap:** Only the Authority question received an affirmative response ("C"); the other two are silence-as-affirmance under ADR-100 rule 2's own route-level convention. This is procedurally compliant with the rule just adopted, but the rule itself governs exactly the kind of self-referential question this is: whether reduced-visibility triage is the right default is, in part, a question about how much gets surfaced as a required ruling versus an overridable default — and this gate answered that question using the very convention under review. The R1 framing audit's own Question 3 inversion named this precise risk (driving-mode defaults applied to a reflexive, building cycle) as the mechanism that produced the MODEL-phase harm findings ADR-100 now cites as its own evidence. Resolving the driving-vs-building pivot itself via a driving-mode-style silent default is a subtler instance of the same pattern, not a departure from it.
- **Recommendation:** No process change is required — the mechanism worked as specified. But the gate note's "Settled premises" list should distinguish, in its own text, which lines rest on an explicit practitioner statement and which rest on non-override of a stated default, so a future reader (or the practitioner revisiting this gate) does not read uniform confidence into two epistemically different kinds of commitment.
