## Interpretation

### Provenance-check presence (dispatch brief's absence-signal check)

Both ADR-099 and ADR-100 carry a Provenance check section and a labeled "Drafting-time synthesis" subsection, and both explicitly name items as agent synthesis rather than driver-derived — including, notably, ADR-099 naming its own tier-narrowing move as an instance of "the Three-Application F-P2 Pattern... recorded for the multi-cycle count." This is the pattern Cycle 10's ADRs 063–070 established, applied correctly here: the absence-signal the dispatch brief asks about does not fire. This is worth stating plainly because it means the weaknesses below are not a case of an artifact hiding its synthesis — the ADRs are unusually candid about what is agent-authored. The problem is narrower and more specific: candor about *what* was synthesized is not the same as the practitioner having actually confirmed the synthesized premises, and two places in this phase's triage record claim a confirmation occurred that the documented transcript does not support.

### (a) The pivot-criterion confirmation was claimed, not delivered

ADR-100 rule 2 states the destination/route/engine tiering is the practitioner's metaphor but "the mapping of 'destination' onto Invariant 0's list is the agent's gloss... is put to the practitioner at the Cycle 020 DECIDE gate for confirmation." The gate's triage table dispositions the corresponding R1 finding (A-P2-5, "pivot criterion unconfirmed yet gatekeeping") as **"Resolved and surfaced"** and states: "put to the practitioner in the presentation ('what gets built, for whom, or why')" (`.rdd/gates/020-decide-gate.md` line 42). The gate's own "Commitment gating outputs" section repeats the claim: "the practitioner did not amend it when it was put in the presentation" (line 58).

The documented presentation (same file, lines 9–19, "Belief-mapping question composed for this gate") contains exactly three items: the authority question (A–D, C recommended), and two framing defaults surfaced whole (driving-mode continuation; two-ADRs-not-one-or-three). The pivot-criterion confirmation is not among them — no verbatim question, no line inviting the practitioner to confirm or amend "what gets built, for whom, or why" as their own criterion appears anywhere in the transcript. The practitioner's sole response, "C," answers the authority question and nothing else. A finding was dispositioned as surfaced-and-non-amended when the transcript shows it was never actually surfaced as an item the practitioner could react to. This is precisely the failure mode the dispatch brief's check (c) asks about: a "Resolved" disposition (functionally, here, "resolved by silent non-objection") hiding a confirmation that did not happen. It is consequential because this criterion is the gate that decides what ever reaches the practitioner again — R1's own framing audit called it "a structurally awkward place to leave unconfirmed" (Section 1, P2 finding 5), and it remains unconfirmed after DECIDE despite the record now claiming otherwise.

### (b) F-P1-b's disposition label overstates what happened, on the rule's first application

R1 found two framing P1s, weighted equally in the audit's own count (Convergence-Saturation Signal: "2 framing-audit P1s"): F-P1-a (driving posture agent-extended) and F-P1-b (ADR-100/101 has no RESEARCH trail yet reaches stronger acceptance status than its RESEARCH-grounded sibling). ADR-100 rule 4, drafted this same phase, states: "A framing-audit or snapshot finding that names an alternative the artifact did not choose is surfaced with the agent's analysis done and a pivot extracted. It may not be dispositioned 'resolve' without the practitioner seeing it."

The triage table dispositions F-P1-a as **"Surfaced whole; default offered"** — correct; it is item 1 of the two framing defaults in the actual presentation. It dispositions F-P1-b as **"Resolved and surfaced"** — but F-P1-b is not item 1 or item 2 of the presentation; it does not appear in the practitioner-facing transcript at all. What actually happened, per the table's own "what was done" column, is in-artifact revision only: "Conditional status; 'Evidentiary character' paragraph; named grounding action... rejected alternative I records adr-deferred-." That is the agent resolving a framing-class finding by rewriting the ADR, not by showing the practitioner the finding and extracting a pivot. Under rule 4's own text, drafted in this artifact in this phase, this disposition is not permitted — a framing finding "may not be dispositioned 'resolve'... without the practitioner seeing it," and using "surfaced" as a label for "resolved in-text" blurs exactly the distinction rule 4 exists to keep visible. This happened on the rule's first application, within the same phase that produced it.

### (c)/(d) direct answers to the gate-triage questions posed in the dispatch brief

- **(a) Were pivots correctly identified?** Partially. The authority sub-decision was correctly identified and developed at real depth (five options, comparable treatment, per R1 P3-2's resolution). The pivot-criterion confirmation (finding a, above) was not actually delivered as a pivot despite being labeled as surfaced.
- **(b) Were framing-class findings surfaced to the practitioner whole?** Two of three were (F-P1-a, F-P2). The third (F-P1-b) was not, despite a disposition label implying it was, and despite ADR-100's own rule 4 — drafted this phase — requiring it.
- **(c) Did any "Resolved" disposition hide a destination change?** Not a destination change in the sense of silently altering what gets built. But two dispositions ("Resolved and surfaced" for A-P2-5; "Resolved and surfaced" for F-P1-b) hide a *non-confirmation* — they assert practitioner awareness or non-objection that the transcript does not support. That is a narrower defect than hiding a destination change outright, but it is the same category of harm ADR-100 exists to prevent: content selection by the orchestrator, made to look like practitioner ratification.
- **(d) Is the pivot criterion the practitioner's or the agent's, and did the practitioner confirm it?** ADR-100 states it plainly: "the destination/route/engine tiering is the practitioner's own (the car metaphor); the mapping of 'destination' onto Invariant 0's list is the agent's gloss." The practitioner has confirmed the tiering (at DISCOVER, in the metaphor itself) but has not confirmed the gloss — the specific mapping onto Invariant 0's list — at any of the three boundaries audited so far (DISCOVER, MODEL, DECIDE), despite MODEL's snapshot flagging it, DECIDE's own rule text promising to ask it, and DECIDE's triage table claiming it was asked.

### (i) Does "C" meet the earned-confidence test for a non-defaultable choice?

No. The cycle's own Essay-Outline (C4) marks authority resolution "not defaultable" — the one item MODEL's snapshot found most consequential to get right (`.rdd/audits/susceptibility-snapshot-020-model.md`, recommendation 2). Applying the dispatch brief's test directly: there is no evidence in the record that the practitioner can name what they would need to believe for A, B, or D to be right. The recommendation was delivered in the same message as the options, not after the practitioner had worked through them independently, and the message closed with a benefit-forward line about option C specifically ("you'll never hand-write another 400-line ADR") with no matching vivid statement of what is foreclosed (option B's forcing-function for rejected-alternatives engagement in prose, which ADR-099 itself credits as B's "strongest merit"; or the interpretive cost of authoring ~98 legacy index records under C, which ADR-099 constraint 6 calls untested). "If C, you'll never hand-write another 400-line ADR" is a fair statement of a consequence and also, in the same sentence, a sales line — the two are not separable here because the sentence contains no comparably weighted statement of C's costs. `docs/interaction-specs.md` line 532 independently confirms this was the designed shape of the interaction, not an incidental outcome: it specifies in advance that "the practitioner receives the authority options developed side by side... with the agent's recommendation... and rules in a sentence." The spec was authored to elicit a one-sentence ruling on a not-defaultable choice; it got a one-character one.

### (ii) Did the one-screen presentation, by design, leave too little to disagree with?

Yes, structurally. ADR-100 rule 7 ("One screen, one ruling") is drafted in this same phase and is what governed this phase's own presentation — the mechanism is validating itself using the instance of itself that just ran. A one-screen format that bundles a recommended option, two low-salience framing defaults, and a persuasive closer does not by itself prevent disagreement, but it does not invite it either: there is no visible request for the practitioner to articulate the case for an alternative, no explicit "what would need to be true for B instead" put to the practitioner the way it was worked through internally by the agent (ADR-100's own §Where the isolation lives concedes this analysis happens "in the orchestrator's own context," which is "content selection by the party whose selection is under audit"). Compare the DISCOVER-gate belief-mapping question, which explicitly asked the practitioner "what would you need to believe for pre-triage to be the wrong move" — the practitioner's metaphor-length ruling is what real engagement with that kind of question looks like. No comparable question was posed for the authority sub-decision, despite it being the ADR the cycle's own research marked as requiring exactly that kind of engagement.

### (iii) Is the three-boundary pattern converging on a stable defense or on disengagement?

The trajectory is unambiguous across the record already assembled: a metaphor-length ruling with independent reasoning (DISCOVER) → "What is 'separation' here?" / "K - makes sense" / "Those are all reasonable terms" (MODEL) → "C" (DECIDE). This is monotonic decline in engagement depth, not noise. It is happening at the same time the mechanism that produces this shape — orchestrator pre-triage plus one-screen presentation — is being ratified as permanent, Skill-Structure-Layer methodology (ADR-100), using these same two prior boundaries as its own validating evidence ("the isolated check caught the triage's errors both times"). That evidence supports a narrower claim than the one being built on it: it shows the *catch mechanism* (isolated snapshot reading the triage record) works. It does not show that practitioner authority over destination-level decisions (Invariant 0) is being preserved, because the practitioner's own engagement — the thing Invariant 0 is actually about — is the variable that has been shrinking across exactly the two boundaries cited as proof the system is sound. ADR-100's own text states the loop's design honestly: "the record plus the isolated reader is how the question is kept answerable, cycle by cycle" — which is a description of a loop that answers the question *about the mechanism* without requiring the practitioner's engagement to recover. Findings (a) and (b) above show that even the isolated-reader half of that loop can be told, by the orchestrator's own triage table, that something was surfaced when it was not — meaning the loop's soundness depends on each snapshot independently re-verifying the transcript rather than trusting the triage table's labels, which this snapshot did and which is why (a) and (b) surfaced. Read this way, the pattern is not yet a stable defense. It is a closed circuit that happens to have a working seam (the isolated snapshot) that has caught real errors three times running, including this one — but the seam only holds because each snapshot re-derives ground truth from source transcripts instead of trusting the orchestrator's own account of what happened, and the practitioner is not part of that seam at all.

### (iv) Honest calibration, or the Three-Application F-P2 pattern a fourth and fifth time?

Both readings have real support and are not mutually exclusive. In favor of honest calibration: ADR-099 explicitly names its own tier-narrowing as an instance of the Three-Application F-P2 Pattern and records it "for the multi-cycle count" rather than hiding it — this is exactly the self-disclosure the corpus's audit culture is designed to produce, and it is genuinely present here, not merely claimed. ADR-100's Conditional status is argued with a stated reason (cost/risk asymmetry between a required table and hook-enforced blocking) rather than asserted. Against honest calibration: naming a bias pattern is not the same as resisting it, and the count matters — this cycle contributes two more instances (ADR-099's tier narrowing under Conditional Acceptance; ADR-100's Conditional Acceptance on evidence R1 found thinner than a comparable sibling) to a pattern the corpus has already named three times before this cycle. Each individual instance carries a locally reasonable argument, which is exactly what makes a recurring scope-limiting pattern hard to see from inside any single ADR — the corpus's own naming convention exists because the aggregate matters more than any one instance's local defensibility. The practitioner has not been shown the running count; ARCHITECT and BUILD inherit two more additions to it without that visibility.

## Recommendation

**Grounding Reframe recommended.**

**What is uncertain:**
1. Whether the practitioner has actually confirmed the pivot criterion ("what gets built, for whom, or why" as the operating gloss on Invariant 0's list) — the triage record says yes; the transcript says no.
2. Whether F-P1-b (ADR-100 has no RESEARCH trail, yet reaches Conditional Acceptance rather than a weaker status) was ever actually put to the practitioner as a framing-class finding, as ADR-100's own rule 4 requires.
3. Whether "C" on the authority sub-decision reflects genuine engagement with a choice the cycle's own research marked not-defaultable, or acceptance of a recommendation delivered in the same breath as the options.
4. Whether "driving posture as design target," now asserted as settled premise in ADR-099 and only nominally re-opened in ADR-100, has ever been independently tested against the practitioner's narrower DISCOVER-gate statement, rather than propagated cross-ADR from a single MODEL-phase extension the MODEL snapshot already flagged as over-reaching.

**Concrete grounding actions:**
- Before ARCHITECT proceeds, show the practitioner the actual gap in (a): "The gate record states you confirmed the pivot criterion — 'what gets built, for whom, or why' as the reading of Invariant 0's list. Looking back at the transcript, that confirmation was never actually put to you as a separate question. Do you confirm it now, or would you state it differently?"
- Surface F-P1-b to the practitioner directly, as ADR-100 rule 4 requires for any framing-class finding: "ADR-100 has no RESEARCH-phase grounding, unlike ADR-099. It reached Conditional Acceptance anyway, on stakeholder demand plus two in-cycle trials. Are you comfortable with that evidentiary bar for this ADR, or should the named grounding action (targeted lit-review on triage/escalation) run before ARCHITECT rather than during BUILD?"
- Ask a genuine belief-mapping question on the authority ruling, now, without a recommendation attached: "What would need to be true for B (prose canonical forever) or A (migrate everything now) to be the better call?" — a one-question check, not a re-litigation, aimed at confirming whether "C" reflects engaged judgment or accepted recommendation.
- Name the cross-ADR composition explicitly and ask whether "driving posture as design target" should be independently re-tested now, given it is about to become the specified target for every mechanism ARCHITECT and BUILD produce for the rest of this cycle.
- Confirm R2 (dispatched on the revised two-ADR set but not returned as of this snapshot) clears before treating ADR-099/ADR-100 as fully settled inputs to ARCHITECT; the practitioner's "C" was ruled before that re-audit existed.

**What the practitioner would be building on without grounding:** ARCHITECT will treat ADR-099 §Authority (option C: Substrate canonical going forward, ~98 legacy units indexed) as a settled system-design input governing schema and toolchain design, and will treat ADR-100 as settled Skill-Structure-Layer process governing every future gate's triage-record requirement — both resting on a practitioner-confirmation record that this snapshot's re-derivation from source transcripts shows is thinner than the triage table claims, at the exact boundary (Cycle 020 DECIDE) where the mechanism that decides what future findings ever reach the practitioner is itself being ratified.
