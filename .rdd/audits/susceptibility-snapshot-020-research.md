## Interpretation

### What the signals collectively suggest

The pattern across Cycle 020's RESEARCH phase is a moderate framing-adoption signal — stronger than earned-confidence but lighter than the sycophantic-reinforcement pole. The specific character is: the research examination was procedurally rigorous (lit-reviews run, alternatives named, audit rounds completed, scope qualifications applied) but the architecture class was never genuinely threatened. The user's structural intuition at Step 1.4 (graph as dense substrate behind human-friendly surface) was confirmed rather than investigated.

The most useful comparison is with the Cycle 019 RESEARCH snapshot's distinction between earned confidence and premature convergence. In Cycle 019, the gate revealed that the pre-gate Essay-Outline did not capture the practitioner's actual epistemic state — the practitioner demanded external evidence the essay had not pursued. In Cycle 020, the gate conversation did not produce an equivalent rupture. The gate responses engaged four framing audit findings (F-P1 through F-P4) and resolved three of them through revisions; F-P2 was explicitly endorsed as skipped. This is a gate that approved rather than substantially redirected the research direction.

The signals that distinguish this from earned confidence:

**1. Single-corpus empirical evidence for a methodology-wide commitment.** C1's topology hypothesis was confirmed on Cycle 019's 5 ADRs. C4's design constraints were surfaced on the same 5 ADRs. The methodology-wide amendment scope in C2/C3 rests on literature mechanism (Sweller's CLT) plus that single corpus. Loop 4 would have provided a second, independent corpus-level data point and was skipped. Whether the skip is defensible research judgment or a momentum-driven decision that made the commitment easier to confirm is not determinable from the artifact alone — but the consequence (methodology-wide scope from single-cycle evidence) is a scope-of-claim gap the gate accepted.

**2. Architecture-class alternatives not empirically tested.** The gate-conversation-tooling alternative (F-P2) was the one architecture-class alternative the audit machinery persistently surfaced across three rounds. The gate endorsed skipping it. The research log's Loop 1.6 argued that scaffolded consolidation is necessary-but-not-sufficient; this is the closest the cycle comes to engaging a simpler alternative. But Loop 1.6 addresses a different alternative (thematic consolidation) and the argument for substrate-necessity was made quickly. The gate-conversation-tooling alternative — skill-text changes co-locating cross-references at gate phases — is a genuinely different architecture class that the Chandler & Sweller 1992 split-attention literature directly supports, and it was not examined before the architecture was committed.

**3. User's telos as an un-stress-tested evaluation criterion.** The telos statement at Step 1.5 frames the cycle's evaluation criterion in a way that most clearly favors the substrate-and-surface architecture (multi-resolution viewing; agent-side preservation; derivation from single source). The telos was accepted as the cycle's load-bearing criterion without examining whether the telos itself could be served by simpler means. The research-design-review-020's incongruity flag (P1-B) noted this pattern but the Loop 1.6 threshold question was answered quickly in the substrate mechanism's favor.

**What is consistent with earned confidence:**

The audit machinery operated correctly and produced real improvements. The W5.4 addition (both readings of the lineage framing presented as coherent; E5.4.2 linking the spike's sub-argument compression-loss to the alternative reading) is a genuine epistemic improvement, not cosmetic. F-P3's CLT softening is accurate. F-P4's SYNTHESIS revision is substantively better. The scope qualifications throughout the artifact (49% figure qualified; CLT as sufficient not unique; single-cycle topology evidence named) are honest scope-of-claim work. The C7 kitchen-sink caveat applying the pattern's own discriminating-criterion risk back to the architecture is genuine held uncertainty.

The research phase produced a well-formed artifact. The concern is not that the artifact is sloppy; it is that the architectural direction was settled at Step 1.4–1.5 and subsequent research confirmed rather than stress-tested it.

The Cycle 019 snapshot identified a "crystallization risk phase (R2 → gate)" where the pre-gate convergence was premature relative to the practitioner's actual epistemic state, with the gate demanding external evidence that corrected the convergence. In Cycle 020, no equivalent correction occurred at the gate. The gate approved the architecture's direction, endorsed F-P2 skip, and triggered three targeted revisions (F-P1, F-P3, F-P4). This pattern is closer to ratification than to challenge.

The Cycle 020 outcome raises the question of whether the DISCOVER phase inherits a direction that was confirmed rather than examined — which is the appropriate grounding question at this boundary.

## Recommendation

**Grounding Reframe recommended — targeted, not cycle-blocking.**

Two signals meet the specificity threshold for grounding actions. One is an architectural alternative that the research explicitly declined to examine before committing; the other is a scope-of-claim gap that downstream phases will inherit if not named.

### Grounding Action 1: Examine the gate-conversation-tooling alternative before DISCOVER commits to the substrate architecture as background

**What is uncertain:** The cycle's central commitment (substrate-and-surface separation as methodology-wide architectural commitment, C3) was made without comparing it against a targeted gate-prompt-tooling intervention — skill-text changes that co-locate cross-references at each gate phase rather than changing the corpus architecture. The Chandler & Sweller 1992 split-attention literature (E2.2.3 in the Essay-Outline itself) identifies co-location as the structural remedy for the split-attention cost. Co-location is achievable at the prompt-construction level without substrate architecture changes. The argument audit surfaced this as F-P2 across three rounds; the gate endorsed skipping it.

**Why it meets the significance properties:**
- Specific: the gate-conversation-tooling alternative is named in the audit record (F-P2, three rounds); the affected artifact decisions are C2's methodology-wide scope and C3's architectural commitment; the DISCOVER value tensions the Essay-Outline §9 names (substrate-authoring-overhead vs. corpus-overwhelm; authority-question-choice vs. authoring-practice) would all be different if the intervention were gate-level rather than architectural.
- Actionable: a concrete comparison can be composed: "What does co-locating cross-references at gate prompts provide that a substrate substrate architecture provides, and what does it fail to provide that the substrate architecture handles?" The research log's Loop 1.6 rationale (multi-resolution viewability and agent-side preservation that skill-text changes cannot deliver) is the starting point but was not subjected to examination at the level it deserves given F-P2's persistence.
- In-cycle applicable: DISCOVER's value tensions and stakeholder maps are not yet written; the tension framing will differ depending on whether the intervention is architectural or gate-level.

**Grounding action:** At DISCOVER entry, before the value-tension and stakeholder-map work begins, surface this to the practitioner: "The research committed to substrate-and-surface separation as the architectural amendment without comparing it against targeted gate-prompt co-location. The Loop 1.6 argument was that the substrate provides multi-resolution viewability and agent-side preservation that skill-text changes cannot — but this was stated rather than examined. Does your experience with the gate-conversation friction (the 'invariant 9 + ADR-014 §C.4' example) confirm that the friction is architectural, or is it possible that better gate-prompt co-location in the existing artifact form would resolve it without corpus restructuring?" The answer will either confirm the architectural direction (which earns confidence the research phase did not earn) or identify a simpler intervention that changes the DISCOVER value-tension framing.

**What is being built on without grounding:** If DISCOVER proceeds to value-tension mapping under the substrate-architecture frame without examining the gate-tooling alternative, MODEL will build domain vocabulary (Substrate-and-Surface Separation; Per-Type Record Schema; Note Discipline; Authority Resolution) for an architectural commitment that was never compared against its primary alternative. DECIDE will draft ADR-CANDIDATE-020.1 through 020.4 against an unexamined premise. The gate-tooling alternative could resolve the user's primary friction (the gate-conversation cross-referential density) at the skill-text layer and defer the architectural commitment to a cycle with more cross-cycle empirical data. Whether that is correct or not is a gate-level judgment — but the practitioner should make it with the alternative explicitly in view, not after skipping its examination.
